## Summary

On June 1, 2026, the U.S. Department of Commerce issued guidance clarifying that export licensing requirements for advanced AI chips apply to any company headquartered or majority-owned by a Chinese parent — regardless of where that subsidiary operates. The rule change closes a gap that had emerged after the Trump administration suspended enforcement of the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule in May 2025, during which an estimated hundreds of thousands of Blackwell-class chips reportedly transited Southeast Asian subsidiaries of Chinese firms. The updated Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) framework shifts the compliance trigger from geography to ownership.

## Key data points

- **Effective date:** June 1, 2026 (guidance issued June 1; enforcement immediate per DoC)
- **Scope:** All companies with HQ or parent incorporated in China — worldwide subsidiaries now require export licenses for Blackwell, Rubin, and AMD MI350x
- **Prior gap:** AI Diffusion Rule suspended May 2025; no clear ownership-based trigger had been in force
- **Loophole scale:** Estimates of hundreds of thousands of advanced chips reaching Chinese entities via Southeast Asian subsidiaries (DoC admission per FDD analysis, June 2, 2026)
- **NVIDIA self-disclosed:** Q1 FY2027 press release (May 28, 2026) stated Q2 FY2027 outlook assumes "no data center compute revenue from China" — management anticipated the tightening
